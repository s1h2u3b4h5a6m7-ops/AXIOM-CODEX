# 📊 Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension **1–5** (1 = weak / high risk, 5 = exceptional). Multiply by the weight,
sum, and divide by the maximum to get a normalized conviction score. Adjust the weights to fit
your strategy.

| Dimension            | Weight | What a 5 looks like                                        |
| -------------------- | :----: | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business quality     |   3    | Simple, profitable, expanding economics                    |
| Moat / durability    |   3    | Wide moat that is clearly widening                         |
| Market opportunity   |   2    | Large, growing, and winnable                                |
| Management & alignment |  2    | Proven operators with real skin in the game                |
| Financial strength   |   2    | Strong cash generation, healthy balance sheet              |
| Valuation / margin of safety | 3 | Priced well below a conservative estimate of value      |
| Downside protection  |   2    | Limited permanent-loss risk even if the thesis is wrong    |

**Score = Σ(rating × weight) ÷ (5 × Σ weights)** → a value between 0 and 1.

## Reading the score
- **≥ 0.80** — High conviction. Size accordingly.
- **0.60–0.79** — Interesting. Watchlist; wait for a better price or more evidence.
- **< 0.60** — Pass, or revisit only if the situation changes materially.

> [!warning] Guard against false precision
> The number is a discipline, not an oracle. If a single dimension scores a 1, ask whether that
> alone is disqualifying regardless of the total.
